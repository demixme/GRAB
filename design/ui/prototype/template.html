  • Change anything: the header counts unsaved changes and Save to device lights up. Revert puts it all back.
  • Hover a curve or its chip: it comes forward with its handle, ready to drag.
  • Drag a dot up or down. Hold Ctrl or Cmd for fine steps. On the RISE, Strike, Press and Lift have square end dots for min and max.
  • Pick a preset, then change something: the preset shows Edited.
  • Switch MIDI mode to Multi: MPE zone and member channels give way to MIDI channel.
  • Turn Autosave on: changes go to the device as you make them, so nothing waits to be saved.
  • Narrow 600: the window scrolls and each settings tile folds, showing a summary when closed.
  • Double-click or right-click any setting, knob or handle to put back the device value.
  • Play a demo phrase, or press and drag on the key strip: dots show where and how hard each finger is, and the graph marks where your input lands on the selected curve.
  • Click the device to open Live keys: a big view of the keys with a readout per note, in place of the settings.
  • Block cluster: the device tile shows your blocks as connected. Click one to see its settings; a Control Block swaps the graph for its buttons. Each block gets its own unsaved dot, and Save writes them all.

Hierarchy

What sits inside what, and the type role each level gets. One family, three sizes (20, 13, 11); weight and colour make the steps.

{{HIER}}

Rules

  • One typeface for everything in the app. Mono only for logs and raw hex.
  • Four sizes: 20 for knob readouts, 13 for names and reading, 12 for the values inside controls (so a value never looks bigger than its row name), 11 for captions and units. The name GRAB is in the window's title bar, not repeated inside.
  • Steps come from weight and colour: title (600, ink), body (400, ink2; values in ink), caption (11, label).
  • Sentence case everywhere. Capitals only in acronyms and product names: MIDI, MPE, CC, USB, RISE, GRAND.
  • Every number uses tabular figures, so values hold still while you drag.
  • Interface strings are ASCII only (GRAB's rule): a plain hyphen for ranges, "Ctrl or Cmd" instead of a key symbol, no middle dots.
  • GRAB's own row names are title case today ("Pitch Bend Range"); this needs those strings changed in the app.

How the settings tiles are laid out

  • Tiles come from the device's setting groups, in order. Row 1 holds the first two; row 2 the rest.
  • A row of one would look empty, so with three tiles they all share row 1.
  • Keyboard is 236 wide and Ports and power 248; card tiles and the first row share what's left.
  • A device with more settings than fit (LUMI colours, Control Block buttons) scrolls the settings area only.
  • Below 760 px: one column, each tile folds to its header and a summary of its values. MIDI output starts open.
  • Where it scrolls, the scrollbar overlays the 12 px gutter (a 6 px thumb, no track), so tiles keep the same width and equal left and right margins whether or not it shows.
  • Suggested minimum window: 600 x 700 (setResizeLimits). GRAB has no limit today.
  • Title bar: merged into the window (window colour, no separate strip, 40 tall), like current desktop apps. macOS keeps the native traffic lights with a transparent, full-size-content title bar (JUCE has no switch for it: a few lines of Objective-C++ on the peer's NSWindow: titlebarAppearsTransparent, NSWindowStyleMaskFullSizeContentView, hidden title); leave a 78 px inset on the left for the lights. Windows and Linux: setUsingNativeTitleBar (false) and draw the bar in GrabLookAndFeel, minimise / maximise / close on the right, close hover red.
  • Several blocks joined by DNA: the device tile draws the arrangement locked up as connected (about 36 px per block unit, gaps 7% of a unit), the selected block at full strength and the others dimmed (no outline), and everything below is that block's. A block without curves (Control Block) hides the graph row; its tiles take their natural height. Unsaved changes are kept per block; the header counts them all and Save writes them all.
  • Top row: drawing tile (fixed width; a Device / Keys switch at its left edge swaps the drawing for a keystroke map in exactly the same area), device dashboard (name, connection, battery, firmware, settings or block n of m), controls tile (preset, saving, Live / Read / Autosave / Tools) as wide as the inspector. One drawing scale everywhere: each device SVG is 3 units tall, 1 unit = 32 px, so a Seaboard Block is 192 x 96 alone and in a cluster. Nothing resizes, so live touches can be drawn on it.
